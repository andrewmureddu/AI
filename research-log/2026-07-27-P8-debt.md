# 2026-07-27 — P8's debt, half paid: the δa³ law confirmed, the law it grew into not testable

**Worked on:** the re-registration of [D1](../experiments/D1-chart-invariance/)'s
P8 — a debt logged in D1's own README and repeated verbatim in the D3 and D4 logs.
**Change:** the δa³ crossover law moves from *post-hoc consistency check* to
*prospectively confirmed*. The stronger two-variable law it was upgraded into is
recorded as **untested**, not confirmed. A third direction error is on record.

## What I did

D1's P8 registered a crossover off the tricritical line, **got the direction
backwards**, and then — after the first run exposed that — produced
D_× ~ δa³, measured at δa^3.211. D1's README said plainly that this was post-hoc
and "does not count until it is predicted in advance and re-run." Three cycles
later it still had not been.

Rather than re-run the same fit, I registered the **stronger** law the derivation
actually gives. The quartic and sextic terms each reach the rounding threshold at
λ₄ = (c₄D/u\*)^½ and λ₆ = (c₆D²/u\*)^⅓; setting them equal gives

```
        D_x  =  c4^3 / (u* c6^2)
```

so the exponent on c₄ is +3 **and on c₆ is −2, separately** — and the δa³ result
is only the c₆-constant slice, which never touched the second exponent. To keep it
at risk rather than algebra, both coefficients come from the real Blume–Capel free
energy (every order present) by sweeping the crystal-field parameter *a*, so the
model rather than the experimenter fixes them.

## What I found

| | registered | measured | |
|---|---|---|---|
| α on c₄ | 3.00 ± 0.30 | **3.111** | ✅ |
| β on c₆ | −2.00 ± 0.40 | **not evaluable** | — |
| D_×c₆²/c₄³ spread | < 2.0× | **1.41×** | ✅ (weak, 3 points) |
| δa exponent | 3.0 ± 0.4 | **3.128** | ✅ |

**The actual debt is paid**: δa^3.128 prospectively, reproducing the post-hoc
3.211. Honest caveat — δa and c₄ are near-proportional this close to the
tricritical point (c₄ ≈ 0.9·δa), so the first and last rows are nearly the same
measurement rather than two independent ones.

**β is untested, and diagnosing *why* was the interesting part.** Only 3 of 8
registered *a*-values gave a usable crossing. Instrumenting a failing case instead
of re-tuning: for a ≥ 0.339 the local slope **rises from 0.500, peaks at 0.5723
near D ≈ 3×10⁻⁴, and turns back down.** It never reaches 2/3, so there is no
upward crossing of the midpoint to locate.

The turnover sits close to the predicted D_× ≈ 1.7×10⁻⁴ — **the crossover is
happening where the theory says it should.** What fails is that the sextic regime
never gets room to establish before the m⁸ and higher terms take over and drag the
slope down. So the two-regime picture is only *measurable* in a window where D_× is
small enough to leave the plateau space, and that window closes at a ≈ 0.339,
before c₆ has moved appreciably. Across the three usable points c₄ spans **1.20
decades** and c₆ only **0.021 decades (5%)** — the two-variable fit is degenerate,
and a β read off it would be noise.

That is a real limitation of the two-regime picture in an all-orders model, and it
is worth more than the fit would have been: the asymptotic regimes a crossover
argument assumes need not both be reachable.

## Decisions

- **δa³ promoted** from post-hoc to prospectively confirmed. D1's README and the
  registration file both updated.
- **β recorded as untested.** Not confirmed, not refuted. Claiming the
  two-variable law holds on this evidence would be exactly the error the
  re-registration exists to correct.
- **A third direction error, on record.** The first implementation searched for
  the slope crossing the midpoint *downward* when it rises with D; it picked up
  spurious crossings in gradient noise and returned D_× that **fell** as c₄ rose —
  the inverse of the prediction, and the same class of error the original P8 made.
  Caught only because the numbers moved the wrong way. Separately, `eps_hi = 1`
  silently dropped every D above ~10⁻³ from the scan.
- **An estimator improvement identified and deliberately not run.** Defining D_×
  as the **location of the slope maximum** rather than a midpoint crossing would be
  well-defined for every *a* and would open the c₆ leverage. That is an estimator
  change made after seeing the data, so it must be registered before it is run.
  Not applying it is the point.

## The pattern worth naming

Three of this register's process failures are now the same shape: **a direction
gotten backwards** (D1's P5, D1's P8, and this script), and two more are **silent
zeros** (D3's tie-broken +1.00, D4's empty fit returning 0.0000). Every one was
caught by a number looking wrong — too round, or moving the wrong way — and none
by the pre-registration itself. The registration catches *conceptual* errors
(what counts as evidence); it does not catch *implementation* errors. Those need
the separate habit of asking, of every result, "which way should this move, and by
how much?" before reading the fit.
