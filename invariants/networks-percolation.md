# Networks & percolation

> **One-line claim:** Connectivity structure (degree distribution, clustering,
> path length) and the percolation transition to a giant connected component recur
> across technological, biological, and social networks.
> **Headline correspondence level:** L3 for percolation/threshold phenomena;
> L2 for structural motifs. *(provisional — seed)*
> **Status:** seed
> **Floor:** **3** (Φ-singular) — connectivity facts are Φ-boundary facts ([S27](../derivations/S27-control-split.md)); the Potts q→1 non-analyticity is systematic

## Statement

A network is a graph; key invariants are the degree distribution, the small-world
property (short average paths + high clustering), and the percolation threshold at
which a giant component appears. Domain-neutral object: the giant-component
transition and epidemic/robustness thresholds derived from it.

## Manifestations by domain (seed)

- CS/infrastructure: internet, power grid, WWW — L3 (percolation robustness).
- Epidemiology: contact networks, epidemic threshold — L3 (percolation-mapped).
- Neuroscience: connectomes, small-world topology — L2/L3.
- Social science: acquaintance networks, "six degrees" — L2.
